# Confidentiality, disclosure timeline, and current rules

Read this reference before external research with unpublished details, public sharing, or describing a package as ready for submission-stage professional review.

## Disclosure timeline

Record known dates and disclosed content for:

- papers, theses, preprints, standards contributions, websites, repositories, videos, demonstrations, competitions, exhibitions, sales, offers, customer deliveries, and presentations;
- earlier patent applications and priority claims;
- disclosures made by the applicant, inventors, collaborators, employers, customers, or third parties;
- confidentiality agreements and access restrictions.

Do not assume a disclosure is harmless, confidential, within an exception, or attributable to the correct party. Flag it for current official-law and patent-professional review when it could affect novelty, priority, ownership, or filing strategy.

## Confidentiality controls

- Keep unpublished applications, drawings, applicant data, customer data, credentials, internal URLs, and machine-specific paths out of public repositories and public sharing services.
- Use abstracted, minimum-necessary technical features for external searching when the user has not authorized disclosure of the complete invention.
- Separate reusable drafting methodology from case-specific confidential materials.
- Before any external upload, submission, publication, email, or repository push, confirm that the exact action and destination are within the user's request.
- Preserve originals and prior delivered versions; use separate generated outputs instead of overwriting the only copy.

## Current-law check

Legal provisions, examination guidance, forms, fees, deadlines, electronic-delivery rules, and online-filing constraints can change. Before making a current procedural statement or delivering a submission-stage package:

1. identify the rule and effective date needed for the task;
2. check current official sources such as the National People's Congress, State Council, CNIPA, and the current official filing system;
3. distinguish binding law or regulation, examination guidance, filing-system limitation, agency preference, and internal quality convention;
4. record the source and access date in the working review record;
5. escalate unresolved legal interpretation to a licensed patent professional.

Do not copy deadlines or filing-system specifications from another skill, blog, old application, or memory without current verification.

Official starting points include the current [Patent Law](https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308800.html), the [2023 revision decision for the Implementing Regulations](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202312/content_6921633.htm), and the [CNIPA order for the Examination Guidelines amendments effective in 2026](https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2025/11/14/art_99_202568.html). Treat these links as starting points, not as a substitute for checking whether a newer official text applies on the task date.

## Applicant and inventor data

Do not infer the applicant, owner, inventors, contribution proportions, addresses, priority claim, or signing authority. Treat inventorship as a human-contribution determination requiring applicant and patent-professional confirmation. Do not list an AI system as an inventor.

## Filing boundary

Generating a technically strong document does not authorize submission. Filing can affect rights, deadlines, public disclosure, inventorship, ownership, and fees. Do not submit, pay, sign, confirm service, or make representations to an authority unless the user explicitly requests that exact external action and all required applicant decisions are available.

Use **package for pre-filing professional review** rather than “directly submittable” when official format, current procedural rules, applicant identities, ownership, inventorship, priority, confidentiality review, signatures, or blocking findings remain unresolved.
